Course Description

Advanced Business Statistics develops the reasoning and techniques a business graduate needs to turn data into defensible decisions. Building on introductory statistics, the course moves from the logic of inference to the full toolkit of applied analysis: describing and visualising data honestly, modelling uncertainty with probability distributions, estimating population quantities, testing hypotheses about one and two groups, comparing several groups with the analysis of variance, measuring categorical association, and modelling relationships with correlation and multiple regression — closing with a framework for choosing the right technique for any business question.

The course is concepts-first and software-agnostic: every method is taught through its statistical logic, its assumptions, a worked business example, and an interpretation guide, so that students can read and defend output from any package rather than merely operating one. Throughout, the emphasis is on honest inference — stating assumptions, quantifying uncertainty, and refusing to overclaim causation from association.

The syllabus is organised into three six-week grading periods aligned to the Philippine Preliminary–Midterm–Final cycle, delivering twelve session topics, three major long quizzes, and three major examinations, and is anchored by a semester-long Business Statistical Report that carries a real analysis from problem framing to an oral defence.

Companion Interactive ModulesThis syllabus is paired with a full set of interactive HTML chapter modules (Chapters 1–11), each with learning objectives, generated figures, worked examples, a cheat sheet, a self-assessment, a 20-item higher-order thinking test, 20 reinforcement drills, a graded business-case study with model answers, and a curated video explainer for every topic (StatQuest, zedstatistics, Brandon Foltz, and others).
Concepts, Not ClicksNo specific statistical software is required. Students who have access to a spreadsheet, R, Python, JASP, or SPSS are encouraged to reproduce the worked examples, but all assessments can be completed with a scientific calculator and the reference tables provided in each module's cheat sheet.

Learning Outcomes

At the end of the course, the students should be able to:

Knowledge

  1. Explain the logic of statistical inference — populations versus samples, sampling distributions, and the role of the Central Limit Theorem — as the foundation of business decision-making under uncertainty.
  2. Describe the major families of probability distributions (binomial, Poisson, and normal) and identify which model applies to a given business situation.
  3. Distinguish estimation from hypothesis testing, and state the hypotheses, assumptions, degrees of freedom, and interpretation of every inferential technique in the course.
  4. Explain correlation, simple and multiple regression, and the meaning of a partial coefficient, R², adjusted R², interaction, and multicollinearity. Core
  5. Select the appropriate statistical technique for a business question from the data type, the study design, and the assumptions each technique requires.

Skills

  1. Compute and interpret descriptive summaries, confidence intervals, and test statistics by hand, and read the equivalent output produced by statistical software.
  2. Conduct one- and two-sample tests, the analysis of variance, chi-square tests, and regression analyses, checking each technique's assumptions before interpreting the result.
  3. Build and diagnose a multiple-regression model — dummy variables, interaction terms, variance inflation factors, and residual and leverage diagnostics — and interpret every coefficient correctly. Core
  4. Communicate statistical findings to a managerial audience with honest uncertainty, correct data visualisation, and plain-language interpretation.
  5. Produce a complete Business Statistical Report, from problem framing through analysis to a defensible recommendation.

Values

  1. Practise intellectual honesty in analysis — reporting assumptions and uncertainty, acknowledging the limits of what data can support, and never presenting association as causation.
  2. Apply statistical and data ethics: data privacy (R.A. 10173), transparent methods, and the responsible, disclosed use of computing and AI tools. Emphasis
  3. Value rigorous, evidence-based reasoning in Filipino business and nation-building, and commit to lifelong quantitative literacy.

Course Policies

Academic Integrity

All submitted work must be the student's own. All sources — print, online, and AI-assisted — must be properly cited. Fabricating data, copying another's analysis, or misrepresenting results is a breach of academic integrity and is dealt with under RTU policy. In statistics specifically, reporting a result while concealing a violated assumption, a discarded case, or an undisclosed search over models is treated as a methodological breach, not a stylistic choice.

Responsible Use of Computing and AI Tools Emphasis

Students may use spreadsheets, statistical software, and generative-AI tools as aids for computation, checking, and language polishing, and are encouraged to reproduce the worked examples. Such use must be disclosed in every submission, including which tool was used and for what. The statistical reasoning, the choice and checking of technique, and the interpretation and recommendation must be the student's own. Presenting AI-generated analysis as one's own work is not permitted.

Data Ethics and Privacy

Project data involving real persons or organisations must be gathered and stored in accordance with the Data Privacy Act (R.A. 10173): informed consent, minimal collection, anonymisation where possible, and secure handling. Sensitive data must never be used without authorisation.

Attendance, Deadlines, and Inclusion

Attendance and make-up work follow RTU and CHED rules. Late deliverables are accepted with deduction unless prior arrangement is made for a documented reason. The class is a safe space for evidence-based disagreement, and submissions are paperless by default.
